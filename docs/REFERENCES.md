# References

NASA/JPL sources used by Mission Architect. Values marked `game` in code are design assumptions, not NASA data. A human must verify every link before final submission.

1. **NASA NSSDC, Mars Fact Sheet.** <https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/marsfact.html>
   - Supplies: Mars gravity 3.71 m/s2; escape velocity 5.03 km/s; radius 3396.2 km; GM 42,828 km3/s2; solar irradiance ~586-589 W/m2; Earth-Mars distance about 55-400 million km; planetary orbit radii used for the Hohmann reference.

2. **NASA/JPL, Mars 2020 Perseverance quick facts.** <https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/mars_2020/launch/quick_facts/> and <https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/mars_2020/landing/quick_facts>
   - Supplies: Perseverance rover mass 1,025 kg; MMRTG ~110 W; Atlas V 541 class launch; 471 million km trip (July 30 launch); ~2.4 B$ build+launch and ~300 M$ prime-mission operations; 1 Mars year prime mission.

3. **NASA/JPL, Mars 2020 launch period and window.** <https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/mars_2020/launch/mission>
   - Supplies: launch window repeats about every 26 months; 1 Mars year = 687 Earth days (about 668 sols).

4. **NASA/JPL, Mars at a Glance (InSight press kit).** <https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/insight/launch/facts/mars-at-a-glance>
   - Supplies: surface pressure under 1/100 of Earth's.

5. **NASA, Mars Relay Network.** <https://science.nasa.gov/mars/mars-relay-network-update/>
   - Supplies: UHF relay up to 2 Mbps during roughly 8-minute orbiter passes.

6. **NASA NTRS, Mars Perseverance Communications (2021).** <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210010148/downloads/Mars%20Perseverance%20Communications%202021%20revised.pdf>
   - Supplies: direct-to-Earth X-band rates of 160-500 bps.

7. **NASA JPL IPN Progress Report 42-246.** <https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-246/42-246A.pdf>
   - Supplies: Mars Relay Network typical rates 0.1-0.5 Mbps at greatest distance.

8. **Zeitlin et al. / Hassler et al., Curiosity RAD radiation measurements (Science, 2013-2014); SwRI release.** <https://www.swri.org/newsroom/press-releases/swri-led-team-calculates-the-radiation-exposure-associated-trip-mars>
   - Supplies: radiation dose equivalent 1.8 mSv/day in cruise and 0.67 mSv/day on the surface (surface about 0.37x cruise).

9. **NASA, Mars poster (average temperature).** <https://www.nasa.gov/pdf/173749main_Mars_Poster.pdf>
   - Supplies: average surface temperature about -81 F (about -63 C).

10. **NASA NSSDC, Moon Fact Sheet.** <https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html>
    - Supplies: Moon radius 1,737.4 km; GM 4,902.8 km3/s2; Earth-Moon distance 384,400 km; lunar gravity.

Images and data credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech where applicable. Values marked `game` are design assumptions, not NASA data.

## Asteroid mission sources

Numbering here is per mission and matches `docs/MISSION_SPEC_ASTEROID.md` section 8.

A1. **NASA, OSIRIS-REx Press Kit (August 2016).** <https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/osiris-rex_press_kit_0.pdf>
   - Supplies: dry mass 880 kg; wet mass 2,110 kg; sample return capsule 46 kg; solar power 1,226-3,000 W; 3.35 m sampling arm; Atlas V 411 class.

A2. **NASA, OSIRIS-REx Fact Sheet.** <https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/osiris_rex_factsheet5-25.pdf>
   - Supplies: launch period; the spacecraft orbited the Sun for a year before using Earth's gravity assist.

A3. **NASA, Bennu Facts.** <https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/101955-bennu/facts/> and <https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/small-bodies/asteroids/101955-bennu/in-depth>
   - Supplies: Bennu about half a kilometre wide; 4.3 h rotation; 1.2 year orbit; mean distance from the Sun about 168 million km; closest Earth approach about 299,000 km every ~6 years; boulders up to 22 m; returned sample 121.6 g.

A4. **NASA, OSIRIS-REx mission page (TAG, sample timeline).** <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/osiris-rex/>
   - Supplies: TAG on 20 October 2020; mission requirement at least 60 g; 70.3 g found on the outside of the sampler head.

A5. **Beshore et al., "OSIRIS-REx: Sample Return from Asteroid (101955) Bennu", IEEE Aerospace 2015.** <https://ael.gsfc.nasa.gov/sed/content/uploadFiles/publication_files/Beshore%20et%20al%20IEEE%202015.pdf>
   - Supplies: mission cost roughly 800 M$ without launch vehicle; Atlas V about 183.5 M$; cost cap about 850 M$ plus launch vehicle. Needs human verification against NASA release figures.

A6. **NASA, OSIRIS-REx mission timeline (arrival, orbit, return).** <https://science.nasa.gov/mission/osiris-rex/>
   - Supplies: arrival 3 December 2018 matching speed at about 19 km; orbit at about 1.75 km on 31 December 2018; departure 10 May 2021; capsule return 24 September 2023.

Images and data credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech/Goddard/University of Arizona where applicable. Values marked `game` are design assumptions, not NASA data.

A human must verify every link before final submission.
